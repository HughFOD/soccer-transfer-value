What is a Premier League player worth?

A model trained on this season's stats, last season's output across 16 leagues, wages, contracts and each player's Transfermarkt value trajectory prices all 545 of them, from strikers to goalkeepers. Every estimate below is out-of-sample, in today's money.

Which model won

Every candidate scored the same way: 5-fold cross-validation, repeated (3 times for all positions, 5 for the forwards comparison), with hyperparameters tuned inside each training fold, so no score has seen its own test players. The first table scores the live model on every position; the second compares feature sets on the 153 forwards, using the earlier version that predicted value directly. All values are in today's money.

How it works

Data. Transfermarkt squads for all 20 clubs (547 players): market values, contracts, extensions, caps, height, foot and every past valuation. FBref for 2026-27 Premier League stats so far (standard, shooting, playing time, misc), estimated wages, and 2025-26 stats from the Big-5 leagues plus the Championship, League One, Portugal, the Netherlands, Belgium, Scotland, Denmark, Sweden, Turkey, Brazil and MLS. Players were matched across sites by name, birth year and club: 151 of 153 forwards matched. FotMob adds xG, xA, chances created, dribbles, tackles, interceptions, clearances, blocks, recoveries and match ratings for the same 16 leagues, and Transfermarkt adds every player's transfer history and fees.

Audit fixes in this version. Three errors were found and fixed. (1) 35 forwards, including every player at the three promoted clubs, had no 2025-26 stats because only the Big-5 leagues were loaded. (2) Transfermarkt labels active internationals two ways ("Current international" and "National player"); only one was read, so Saka, Rashford and Semenyo counted as non-internationals. (3) Most current values were set in May–July, before this season began; the model now knows each valuation's date, and the calculator prices players as of Transfermarkt's latest update round.

Stats that don't overreact. Per-90 rates pool this season with last season (weighted 0.75) and are shrunk toward the average using a six-match pseudo-sample. A player with two cameo goals can't post an elite rate.

Value trajectory. For each player the model sees his previous Transfermarkt valuation, how long ago it was set, his value 12 months before that, his momentum between the two, and his peak. These are anchored to his own valuation dates. The first version used a fixed "one year ago" date, which hid fast risers: Kroupi went €15m → €22m → €40m → €70m, and the model only saw the €15m. Fixing this cut median error from 23% to 12%.

Inflation. Every past valuation, and every current one set before today, is restated to 16 November 2026 money at 10% a year. That sits between CIES's decade-long Premier League average (about 12% a year) and this summer's record spending growth (about 11%). Testing 0%, 10% and 14% changed accuracy by under a point, because inflation mostly shifts all values together. It matters for reading the numbers: a €40m valuation from 2024 is about €48m today.

Training. An equal blend of LightGBM, ridge regression and Extra Trees, trained on every Premier League player (position flags let it separate roles) and scored on every player with cross-validation. The model predicts how far each player's value will move from his previous Transfermarkt valuation, rather than his value from scratch. That anchors established stars where Transfermarkt already has them: before this change the model had Declan Rice 21% under and Haaland 26% under; now they are within 5%. It also cut the overall median error from 10.4% to 9.3%. Adding Extra Trees as a third model then cut the big misses: mean error 14.3% → 13.8%, and 86% of players now land within ±25%. Repeating each player's previous valuation scores about 18%; the gap is what stats, age, contract and momentum add.

Young players. Players aged 21 or under are priced by a youth model trained on today's squads plus the three previous summers' Transfermarkt revaluations (2,089 extra player-seasons, weighted 0.3). That cut their median error from 17% to 14%; for older players the past seasons made things slightly worse, so they use today's data only. Both models also see injuries (days out this season and last year, injured now) and youth-international careers.

Likely fee. Clubs pay more than Transfermarkt's figure. Across 610 permanent transfers since 2021 involving these players, the typical fee was 1.25× the Transfermarkt value at the time. The multiple is higher for cheaper and younger players and for Premier League buyers, and it levels off at about 1.15–1.3× for players worth over €10m. The Likely fee column applies that to each player's current Transfermarkt value, assuming a Premier League buyer; hover a fee for the range that half of real deals fell inside. For players over €30m a flat ×1.25 is as accurate as the fitted multiple (both are typically about 20% off), so treat the fee as a guide, not a quote. It can't see contract length at the time of past deals, which is a big lever in real negotiations.

Tested and left out. xG and defensive stats improve the stats-only model (25% → 23% median error for forwards) but not the full one, even for defenders. Transfer-fee and breakout signals didn't help predict sudden re-ratings. League-strength scaling of last season's stats made things slightly worse. Each was tested with the same nested cross-validation.

Limits.

  • Sudden jumps can't be foreseen. The biggest gaps are players Transfermarkt re-rated sharply at their latest update: Hemmings (€0.8m → €15m), Emersonn, Belloumi. Kroupi (about a third under) is the clearest case. His value jumped from €40m to €70m in June after a 13-goal season, but he has played 50 minutes this season, so nothing in this season's data backs the jump.
  • Injured stars: a player out for months is marked down, because Transfermarkt cut comparable long-injured players this season (Ekitiké −13%, Simons −22%, Onana −14%). Saliba (back injury since July, no minutes) is about 24% under his €100m valuation. That is a forecast of Transfermarkt's next update, not his price: his Likely fee is about €117m.
  • Goalkeepers on stats alone: the stats-only model is weak for keepers (about 49% median error), so trust the full model's number for them.
  • Early season: the snapshot is about 11 matchdays in, so last season still carries most of the stats signal.
  • The target is Transfermarkt's opinion, not a transfer fee. A "gap" means the model and Transfermarkt disagree, not that a deal is a bargain. The Likely fee column answers the other question: what a club would pay.
  • Wages are FBref's published estimates and cover most players; the model handles missing wages explicitly.
  • Leans on Transfermarkt's own history: the previous valuation carries about 70% of the model's split gain. The stats-only model (about 26% median error across positions) shows what on-pitch numbers say alone.
  • Calculator vs table: the table shows out-of-sample estimates. The calculator uses the final model fitted on everyone, so for a real player it sits closer to his actual value.

What is a footballer worth?

A model trained on this season's stats, last season's output across 16 leagues, wages, contracts and each player's Transfermarkt value trajectory prices all of them across the Premier League, La Liga, the Bundesliga, Serie A and Ligue 1, from strikers to goalkeepers. Every estimate below is out-of-sample, in today's money.

Which model won

Every candidate scored the same way: 5-fold cross-validation, repeated (3 times for all positions, 5 for the forwards comparison), with hyperparameters tuned inside each training fold, so no score has seen its own test players. The first table scores the live model on every position; the second compares feature sets on the 153 forwards, using the earlier version that predicted value directly. All values are in today's money.

How it works

Data. Transfermarkt squads for every club in the Premier League, La Liga, the Bundesliga, Serie A and Ligue 1 (2,641 players): market values, contracts, extensions, caps, height, foot, every past valuation, injuries, national-team careers and transfer fees. FBref for 2026-27 stats so far in all five leagues (standard, shooting, playing time, misc) and estimated wages, plus 2025-26 stats from the Big-5 leagues and 11 more (Championship, League One, Portugal, the Netherlands, Belgium, Scotland, Denmark, Sweden, Turkey, Brazil and MLS). FotMob adds xG, xA, chances created, dribbles, defensive actions and match ratings. Players are matched across sites by name, birth year and club: 92–98% matched to FBref in each league.

Audit fixes in this version. Three errors were found and fixed. (1) 35 forwards, including every player at the three promoted clubs, had no 2025-26 stats because only the Big-5 leagues were loaded. (2) Transfermarkt labels active internationals two ways ("Current international" and "National player"); only one was read, so Saka, Rashford and Semenyo counted as non-internationals. (3) Most current values were set in May–July, before this season began; the model now knows each valuation's date.

Stats that don't overreact. Per-90 rates pool this season with last season (weighted 0.75) and are shrunk toward the average using a six-match pseudo-sample. A player with two cameo goals can't post an elite rate.

Value trajectory. For each player the model sees his previous Transfermarkt valuation, how long ago it was set, his value 12 months before that, his momentum between the two, and his peak. These are anchored to his own valuation dates. The first version used a fixed "one year ago" date, which hid fast risers: Kroupi went €15m → €22m → €40m → €70m, and the model only saw the €15m. Fixing this cut median error from 23% to 12%.

Inflation. Every past valuation, and every current one set before today, is restated to 16 November 2026 money at 10% a year. That sits between CIES's decade-long Premier League average (about 12% a year) and this summer's record spending growth (about 11%). Testing 0%, 10% and 14% changed accuracy by under a point, because inflation mostly shifts all values together. It matters for reading the numbers: a €40m valuation from 2024 is about €48m today.

Training. An equal blend of LightGBM, ridge regression and Extra Trees, trained on all 2,618 valued players in the five leagues (league and position flags let it separate them) and scored with cross-validation. The model predicts how far each player's value will move from his previous Transfermarkt valuation, rather than his value from scratch, which anchors established stars where Transfermarkt already has them. With five times the data it uses bigger trees (16 leaves instead of 4–8). For Premier League players the estimate is the average of this model and the earlier Premier League-only model; averaging the two cut their median error from 9.1% (Premier League-only) and 9.4% (five leagues) to 8.5%, and the mean from 13.7% to 13.4%. Repeating each player's previous valuation scores about 18%; the gap is what stats, age, contract and momentum add.

Young players and past seasons. Version 17 priced under-21s with a youth model that also learned from three past summers of Premier League revaluations. With all five leagues the past summers (10,501 player-seasons) no longer help, even for young players (median error 23% → 26% when added), so this version uses one model for everyone. Young players outside the Premier League remain the hardest group: about 26% median error, against 13% for under-21s in the Premier League.

Likely fee. Across 2,211 permanent transfers since 2021 involving these players, what a club paid depended mostly on who was buying. Premier League buyers paid above Transfermarkt's value at the time: about 1.25× for players worth €10–30m and 1.17× above €30m (more for cheap and young players). Clubs elsewhere paid about Transfermarkt's value or a little less. The Likely fee column applies the fitted multiple (value, age, time at the club, selling league) to each player's current Transfermarkt value, assuming a Premier League buyer; hover a fee for the range that half of real deals fell inside. For players over €30m a flat multiple is as accurate as the fitted one (both typically about 25% off), so treat the fee as a guide, not a quote. It can't see contract length at the time of past deals, which is a big lever in real negotiations.

Underlying metrics (stats view and scouting cards). Up to five seasons per player from FotMob: xG, xA, xG on target, shots, chances and big chances created, dribbles, passes, long balls, tackles, interceptions, clearances, blocks, recoveries, possession won high up, goalkeepers' goals prevented, plus team xG for and against and possession. Rates pool the seasons (this season counts most) and shrink toward the role average so a few good games can't produce an elite number. Attacking output is converted to Premier League terms with factors learned from 285 players who changed league: the same player produces about 27% more xG + xA per 90 in the Bundesliga, 24% more in Ligue 1, 7% more in La Liga and 2% more in Serie A. Defensive counts are possession-adjusted, because a defender on a team that has the ball 65% of the time gets fewer chances to tackle. A two-step projection predicts next season's xG + xA per 90 (R² 0.78 out of sample, against 0.66 for "same as last season"), and an ageing curve says how output typically changes at each age. Together these cut the stats view's median error from 31.5% to 29.5% (Premier League 23.9% → 22.4%). It is a real signal: where the stats view sat more than 50% away from a player's previous Transfermarkt value, Transfermarkt's next update moved toward it 77% of the time (871 players).

Tested and left out. The deep metrics don't improve the main estimate, which leans on Transfermarkt's own history (Premier League 8.5% either way), so they power the stats view and scouting cards instead. Using comparable players' values as an input didn't help (they're shown on the cards for context). FBref's passing, chance-creation, possession and defending tables were collected, but their advanced columns are now blank, so FotMob supplies those numbers. Past seasons as extra training rows, transfer-fee and breakout signals, and Opta league-strength scaling were also tested and didn't help. Everything uses the same nested cross-validation.

Limits.

  • Sudden jumps can't be foreseen. The biggest gaps are players Transfermarkt re-rated sharply at their latest update: Hemmings (€0.8m → €15m), Emersonn, Belloumi. Kroupi (about a third under) is the clearest case. His value jumped from €40m to €70m in June after a 13-goal season, but he has played 50 minutes this season, so nothing in this season's data backs the jump.
  • Injured stars: a player out for months is marked down, because Transfermarkt cut comparable long-injured players this season (Ekitiké −13%, Simons −22%, Onana −14%). Saliba (back injury since July, no minutes) is about 22% under his €100m valuation. That is a forecast of Transfermarkt's next update, not his price: his Likely fee is about €115m.
  • Goalkeepers and centre-backs on stats alone are the hardest to price: goals prevented is noisy over a season, and counting stats can't see a defender's positioning.
  • Early season: the snapshot is about 11 matchdays in, so last season still carries most of the stats signal.
  • The target is Transfermarkt's opinion, not a transfer fee. A "gap" means the model and Transfermarkt disagree, not that a deal is a bargain. The Likely fee column answers the other question: what a club would pay.
  • Wages are FBref's published estimates and cover most players; the model handles missing wages explicitly.
  • The main estimate leans on Transfermarkt's own history: past valuations carry most of its split gain, which is why it is accurate (it mostly learns how Transfermarkt updates itself). The stats view (29.5% median error across the five leagues, 22.4% in the Premier League) is what the underlying numbers alone say.

What is a Premier League forward worth?

A model trained on this season's stats, last season's output across 16 leagues, wages, contracts and each player's Transfermarkt value trajectory prices all 153 of them. Every estimate below is out-of-sample, in today's money.

Which model won

Every candidate scored the same way: 5-fold cross-validation repeated 5 times, with hyperparameters tuned inside each training fold, so no score has seen its own test players. Scores are for the 153 forwards only, against values in today's money.

How it works

Data. Transfermarkt squads for all 20 clubs (547 players): market values, contracts, extensions, caps, height, foot and every past valuation. FBref for 2026-27 Premier League stats so far (standard, shooting, playing time, misc), estimated wages, and 2025-26 stats from the Big-5 leagues plus the Championship, League One, Portugal, the Netherlands, Belgium, Scotland, Denmark, Sweden, Turkey, Brazil and MLS. Players were matched across sites by name, birth year and club: 151 of 153 forwards matched, and only 5 have no senior league minutes last season (youth players, and Mudryk, who didn't play).

Audit fixes in this version. Three errors were found and fixed. (1) 35 forwards, including every player at the three promoted clubs, had no 2025-26 stats because only the Big-5 leagues were loaded. (2) Transfermarkt labels active internationals two ways ("Current international" and "National player"); only one was read, so Saka, Rashford and Semenyo counted as non-internationals. (3) Most current values were set in May–July, before this season began; the model now knows each valuation's date, and the calculator prices players as of Transfermarkt's latest update round.

Stats that don't overreact. Per-90 rates pool this season with last season (weighted 0.75) and are shrunk toward the forward average using a six-match pseudo-sample. A forward with two cameo goals can't post an elite rate.

Value trajectory. For each player the model sees his previous Transfermarkt valuation, how long ago it was set, his value 12 months before that, his momentum between the two, and his peak. These are anchored to his own valuation dates. The first version used a fixed "one year ago" date, which hid fast risers: Kroupi went €15m → €22m → €40m → €70m, and the model only saw the €15m. Fixing this cut median error from 23% to 12%.

Inflation. Every past valuation, and every current one set before today, is restated to 16 November 2026 money at 10% a year. That sits between CIES's decade-long Premier League average (about 12% a year) and this summer's record spending growth (about 11%). Testing 0%, 10% and 14% changed accuracy by under a point, because inflation mostly shifts all values together. It matters for reading the numbers: a €40m valuation from 2024 is about €48m today.

Training. A 50/50 blend of LightGBM and ridge regression on log value, trained on every Premier League player (position flags let it separate roles) and scored only on forwards. The ridge half lets estimates go above the highest values in the training data. Training on all positions beat forwards-only training by about 4 points of median error, because the extra 394 players teach the age, contract and history effects much better. Repeating each player's previous valuation scores 18.2%; the model's 12.2% is the value added by stats, age, contract and momentum.

Limits.

  • Sudden jumps can't be foreseen. The biggest gaps are players Transfermarkt re-rated sharply at their latest update: Hemmings (€0.8m → €15m), Emersonn, Belloumi. Kroupi (−32%) is the clearest case. His value jumped from €40m to €70m in June after a 13-goal season, but he has played 50 minutes this season, so nothing in this season's data backs the jump.
  • Top of the market: Haaland is still 19% under, the most valuable player in the data with nobody above him to learn from. The ridge half of the blend limits this.
  • No xG, xA or passing data: FBref stopped publishing advanced stats, and the free sources don't replace them. A paid API (Sportmonks, TheStatsAPI) would fill this.
  • Early season: the snapshot is about 11 matchdays in, so last season still carries most of the stats signal.
  • The target is Transfermarkt's opinion, not a transfer fee. A "gap" means the model and Transfermarkt disagree, not that a deal is a bargain.
  • Wages are FBref's published estimates and cover 115 of 153 forwards; the model handles missing wages explicitly.
  • Leans on Transfermarkt's own history: the previous valuation carries about 70% of the model's split gain. The stats-only model (27% median error) shows what on-pitch numbers say alone.
  • Calculator vs table: the table shows out-of-sample estimates. The calculator uses the final model fitted on everyone, so for a real player it sits closer to his actual value.
